Design Rationale & UX Improvements
Key decisions and research-backed improvements behind the redesign.
Home — Motivation Engine

Problem: The old home screen was a passive data display with no clear call to action. Stats (0 streak, 380 words, 0 mastered) were demoralizing.

Solution: The hero card now centers on today's actionable goal with a circular progress ring showing completion. The ring fills as you review, creating immediate visual feedback. "Start session" is the primary CTA — one tap to begin learning.

Research: Duolingo and Anki both show that a clear daily goal with visual progress drives 2-3× more daily engagement than passive stats alone.

Home — Word of the Day

Problem: No passive learning opportunities between review sessions.

Solution: A "Word of the Day" card with the accent-colored left border draws attention without being intrusive. It shows the word, translation, grammatical info, and an example sentence in context — reinforcing the i+1 comprehensible input principle.

The play button lets users hear pronunciation without navigating away, reducing friction for audio learning.

Home — Weekly Progress Chart

Problem: Users had no visibility into their learning patterns over time.

Solution: A compact bar chart showing daily review activity for the week. Today is highlighted in accent color, completed days in brand green. A goal line gives users something to work toward. This creates accountability and helps identify patterns (e.g., skipping weekends).

Vault — Smarter Filtering

Problem: The old "der / die / das" pills were cramped and visually noisy. Filter options were limited. The search was basic.

Solution: Filters are reorganized into two tiers. The primary filter row uses chips for learning states (All, Due now, New, Mastered) — the most useful ways to slice vocabulary. Article gender filters move to a subtle legend row below, using colored dots that match the article badge system. A sort dropdown completes the toolbar.

This layered approach puts learning-relevant filters first and article filters second, reflecting actual user behavior.

Word Card — Rich Context

Problem: The old word cards showed only the word and translation — an empty, uninformative layout that wasted space and provided no learning value at a glance.

Solution: Each card now shows five layers of information:

  • Mastery bar — color-coded left accent strip shows SRS state at a glance
  • Word + phonetic — with article badge for nouns
  • Translation + category — context immediately visible
  • Example sentence — one-line preview with the word highlighted in brand color
  • Status footer — mastery level, interval, due badge, and a quick play-audio action

This transforms the card from a label into a learning artifact. Users can recall context without tapping into the detail view.

Word Detail — Card-based Sections

Problem: The old detail page was a single long scroll with divider lines. Mastery stats were small info cells. No related words or learning actions.

Solution: Content is grouped into distinct cards, each with a clear purpose: header (identity), mastery (progress), examples (context), notes (tips), related words (expanding vocabulary), and bottom actions (what to do next). The mastery ring replaces the flat progress bar for a more engaging visualization. A "Review this word" CTA drives action directly from the detail view.

New feature: "Related Words" section leverages existing vocabulary data to show word families, helping learners build connections between words they already know.
